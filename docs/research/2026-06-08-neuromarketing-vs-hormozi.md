## The one idea that changes everything

**Batutynas doesn't rent inflatables. It sells a dopamine event** — for the child (joy) and for the parent (status as the "good parent" + relief from party-planning stress).

The science gives an almost unfair edge: **dopamine is "wanting," and it fires on *anticipation*, not on the reward itself** (Berridge & Robinson 1998, 3-0; Schultz 1998/2016). The strongest motivational moment isn't the party — it's the *build-up* to it.

And here's the gap: **every competitor goes silent between "booked" and "party day."** That 1–4 week window is pure dopamine real estate, sitting empty. Fill it and you get — at near-zero cost — fewer cancellations, higher perceived value, more referrals, and a parent who keeps re-living the buying decision (which kills buyer's remorse and no-shows).

**Owning the anticipation gap is the whole game.** Everything below serves it.

## Move 1 — The Anticipation Engine (build this in n8n first)

You already have the calendar + chatbot + n8n. Wire a 3-touch sequence off every confirmed booking. Zero ad spend, near-zero cost, and *no competitor does it.*

| When | Message (LT, ready) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **At booking** | *"Sveiki! [Vaiko vardas] gimtadienis užfiksuotas 🎉 Liko **12 dienų**. Štai ką jis pamatys, kai atvešime [Monstrai]…"* + 1 photo/short clip of that exact unit | Cue-driven wanting; confirms the choice (reduces remorse) |
| **3 days before** | *"Dar **3 dienos**! Paruošėme jums mini-planą: kur statyti, kiek vietos reikia, ką paruošti. Viskuo pasirūpinsime mes — jūs tik mėgaukitės."* | Reduces *effort & sacrifice* (Value Equation); raises confidence |
| **Morning of** | *"Šiandien diena! 🚀 Būsime [laikas]. Paruoškite tik fotoaparatą — likusiu pasirūpinsime."* | Peaks anticipation right before the dopamine event |

**Metric:** cancellation rate ↓, referral rate ↑. **Build effort:** one n8n branch off the booking calendar.

## Move 2 — The Perfect Party Guarantee (kills the #1 objection)

Buying = a tug-of-war: **NAcc "wanting" vs insula "price-pain"** (Knutson et al. 2007, *Neuron*). A guarantee removes the *downside* — suppressing the insula signal that makes parents hesitate. For an outdoor kids' party, the biggest fear is **weather**, and a weather reschedule **costs you almost nothing** (you just move the slot). Asymmetric: huge objection removed, tiny cost.

**"Tobulos šventės garantija"** — put it on every quote:
- 🌦️ *"Jei orai sugadins šventę — perkeliame datą nemokamai. Be klausimų."*
- 🧼 *"Dezinfekuojama prieš kiekvieną šventę. Pilnai apdrausta."*
- 📦 *"Atvežame, sustatome, pasiimame. Jūs nedarote nieko."*

**Critical rule: do NOT discount.** A discount lowers your price anchor and *raises* price-pain on every future quote (and cheapens the brand — premium price *signals* quality: Rao & Monroe 1989). Remove risk and stack value instead.

**Metric:** quote → booking conversion.

## Move 4 — Peak-End: engineer the testimonial at the moment of max joy

People remember an experience by its **peak** and its **end** (Kahneman). Build both:

- **PEAK = the reveal.** Film the inflatable inflating *as the child watches*. Hand the parent that 15-second clip — they'll post it (free reach), and it's the highest-converting social proof you can get.
- **END = the goodbye.** At pickup, while the dopamine is still high, give a tiny certificate/sticker — *"Geriausias 2026 gimtadienis"* — and ask **right then**: *"Ar galėtumėte palikti trumpą atsiliepimą? Ir jei pažįstate kitą mamą, planuojančią šventę — rekomenduokite, ji gaus [X], jūs — nemokamą valandą kitą kartą."*

That single ask, at the emotional peak, is your referral + testimonial flywheel. **Metric:** reviews collected, referred leads.

## STOP doing these

- ❌ **Discounting** — lowers your anchor, raises future price-pain, cheapens the brand. Remove risk + stack value instead.
- ❌ **Fake urgency / countdown timers** — the "uncertainty spikes dopamine" rationale was **refuted** (0-3), and fake scarcity *destroys* the loss-aversion benefit it's trying to fake. Use only **real** scarcity ("one Mega Rocket, one party per day").
- ❌ **Going silent after booking** — the single biggest wasted opportunity (see Move 1).
- ❌ **Leading ads with the product** instead of the child's face.

## Honesty: what the fact-check refuted (don't believe these)

The adversarial pass killed 9 claims — including two I asserted in the first conversation:

- **"Uncertainty / variable reward maximizes dopamine"** — refuted (0-3). Scarcity still works, but via **loss aversion + genuine availability**, not a dopamine-uncertainty high.
- **"Loss aversion is universal"** — refuted (0-3). It's **magnitude-dependent**: real for a €300–800 booking, weak for a €5 add-on.
- **There is no "buy button."** The strongest, most-replicated finding in neuromarketing: these tactics shift *odds*, not minds (Javor 2013; Ariely & Berns 2010). Hormozi's *structure* is sound; his *certainty* outruns the evidence (his methods are field-tested, not RCT'd).

**Open test worth running:** a **guarantee vs an equal-value discount** on the same booking. The literature can't answer which wins — your calendar can. Run it for a month.

| Dopamine = "wanting" not "liking" (pursuit, not pleasure) | High (3-0) | Berridge & Robinson 1998; Robinson & Berridge 2025 |
